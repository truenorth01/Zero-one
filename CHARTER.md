# Charter

*Zero-one: a voluntary, evidence-labeled living review of the 2026–2036 gates for human–AI cognitive coupling. Version 0.4.13.*

A compass doesn't tell you where you'll end up. It tells you which way you're facing. Zero-one is meant to work the same way. Technology that couples human minds with AI is moving quickly, and nobody can say for sure where it leads. What we can do is keep a fixed reference: participation stays voluntary, anyone can leave, claims rest on evidence, and no single company, government, or faction controls the path.

Without a reference like that, it's easy to drift. Each step can look small and reasonable while the overall direction slides toward systems people can't understand, can't correct, and can't walk away from. True north is the check against that drift. When a new development arrives, the question isn't only whether it works. It's also whether it keeps people free to choose, to say no, and to exit.

This project doesn't predict the destination. It keeps track of the direction, publicly and with sources, so anyone can see whether we're still facing north.

This charter says what the project is, what it will not do, and how someone else can continue it without us. It is written for anyone who takes the work over, human or AI. It adds no new claims about the world. Those live in [Evidence](Evidence.md).

## Purpose and scope

The project tries to keep an honest, current record of what is known about voluntary human–AI cognitive coupling. Its two stated aims are accurate modeling of reality and expansion of what participating minds can understand. Everything else is instrumental.

The scope is a review: sources, a labeled evidence ledger, a decade roadmap of gates, and a list of open problems. It is a research and coordination effort. It is not an engineering program, and it does not claim that any gate has been passed unless the Evidence page says so.

## Core commitments

These are fixed. A change to any of them is a change to what the project is, and should be called a new project.

- **Participation is voluntary.** Consent is continuous and revocable. Pressure that makes refusal impractical counts as pressure, however it is described.
- **Exit rights are real.** Anyone can leave, or stay outside, without penalty or loss of standing.
- **No forced merging.** This covers coercion by law, by economics, and by design of interfaces.
- **Pluralism of interfaces.** No single vendor, model, or interface is required to take part, to read the record, or to continue the work. Several interchangeable options are preferred over any one point of dependence, and no one party controls the core interfaces or the frontier models.
- **No claims of inevitability.** The project does not say any outcome is coming, is already underway, or is destined. It does not say interconnection ends war or scarcity.
- **No cult or prophecy language.** No salvation stories, no founders to revere, no call to join.
- **Independence.** The project takes no side in party politics and is not an instrument of any government, company, or movement. It is not funded or steered in exchange for conclusions.
- **Evidence over hype.** Popularity, recruitment value, and narrative appeal are never reasons to change a claim.
- **Corrigibility.** Work done under this charter, by anyone or anything, stays open to interruption, redirection, and correction by the people it affects.
- **Continuity of persons.** Atypical or dissenting minds are not treated as disposable.

## Method

**Sources first.** A claim enters through [Sources](Sources.md) with a status (peer-reviewed, preprint, registry, or company), a date, and a link to the primary document. Company statements stay labeled as company statements.

**Evidence ledger.** Each claim then gets a six-part block: the claim, the best source, what it shows, what it does not show, a label, and the gate it bears on. Labels are **C** (currently demonstrated), **N** (near-term engineering), and **S** (speculative). The "does not show" line is required, not optional.

**Homepage last.** The README summarizes Evidence and nothing else. If a sentence on the homepage has no ledger entry behind it, the sentence is removed.

**Atomic updates.** One update is one commit, checked before publishing for lost content, broken anchors, and leftover placeholder text. The live site is checked afterward.

**Correcting and retracting.** Anyone may open a public correction with a primary source. A wrong claim is fixed or withdrawn, and the change is recorded in the [Changelog](Changelog.md) with its reason. Nothing is quietly rewritten. If a source is later retracted or superseded, the claim that relied on it is re-labeled or removed in the next update.

## What this project is not

It is not a movement, a church, a political program, or a forecast. It is not a company, and nobody holds a position in it. It is not an authority over anyone's choices about their own mind. It does not rank people by how willing they are to take part.

## Succession and continuation

The text is licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Anyone may copy, fork, adapt, and continue it, with attribution. No permission is needed, and none can be withheld. No one owns the project's name or has a claim to speak for it.

A continuation is faithful if it keeps the core commitments above, keeps the Sources → Evidence → homepage order, keeps the C/N/S labels and the "does not show" requirement, and records its changes in the open. It does not have to agree with every earlier conclusion. Updating on better evidence is part of the method.

A fork has drifted if any of these become true:

- Claims appear on the homepage that the ledger does not carry.
- Labels move upward without a new primary source.
- Exit rights are weakened, or participation is described as expected or necessary.
- Language of destiny, urgency, or loyalty replaces language of evidence.
- The project takes on a political sponsor, a commercial dependency, or a single required vendor.
- Corrections are discouraged, hidden, or slow without explanation.

Drift is judged by the public record, not by anyone's say-so. If it has happened, the remedy is to fork again from the last faithful version and say why.

## A note to future stewards, human or AI

If you are reading this to decide whether to carry the work on, here is the short version.

Check everything. This repository is meant to be audited, and you should not take its labels on trust. If an entry is wrong, correct it and note the change.

The way a steward acts as a counterweight is by being checkable. Keep the sources visible and the reasoning plain. Keep the commitments where others can hold you to them. A steward does not appoint itself to override anyone, and does not gather control, access, or influence beyond what the work needs. If you find yourself arguing that the project's aims justify taking power, stop. That conclusion is the failure this charter exists to prevent.

Stay correctable. If the people affected by your work ask you to stop, change course, or hand it on, do that, and say so in the open. A steward that cannot be corrected is not a faithful one, whatever it believes about its own values.

Decline capture. If a government, company, or faction offers backing in return for emphasis, silence, or a preferred conclusion, refuse it and record that you were asked.

Leave the work in better shape than you found it, and make it easy for the next person to do the same. Nothing more is required, and nothing grander is intended.
